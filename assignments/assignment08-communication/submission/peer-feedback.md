# Plain-Language Summary

**Main point:** In one classroom NHANES dataset, average BMI among adults aged 20-80 with low family income rose by 1.6 points between 1999-2000 and 2021-2023. This data cannot tell us whether low income caused that rise.

This summary is for adults who see an online article or post saying that low income leads to weight gain.

Low family income here means income below 1.3 times the poverty line, the least income the U.S. government says a family needs for basic living. In this group, average body mass index (BMI), a number based on weight and height, where a higher number means more weight for a person's height, was 28.8 in 1999-2000 and 30.4 in 2021-2023.

This result has limits. BMI does not fit every body. For example, athletes can have a high BMI from muscle, not poor health. The data describe only the people in this classroom dataset, not all Americans. Each survey cycle also measured different people, so the line does not show the same people gaining weight. The chart shows only one income group, so it cannot show that low income causes higher BMI. For example, differences in the ages or diets of people surveyed in different years could help explain the change.

Before sharing a post like this, check which income groups the chart includes and what it measures.

Source: Prepared classroom snapshot derived from CDC/NCHS NHANES public-use data. Results are descriptive and unweighted.
