# Plain-Language Summary

**Main point:** In one classroom NHANES dataset, adults aged 20-80 with low family income had a slightly higher average BMI in recent survey years. This shows a pattern, not a cause.

This summary is for members of the general public who see news or social media posts about income and weight.

Low family income here means income below 1.3 times the U.S. poverty line. In this group, average body mass index (BMI) was 28.8 in 1999-2000 and 30.4 in 2021-2023. That is a rise of under 2 units over 11 survey cycles, with a few small dips.

This result has limits. BMI does not fit every body. For example, athletes can have a high BMI from muscle, not poor health. The data describe only the people in this classroom dataset, not all Americans. Each survey cycle also measured different people, so the line does not show the same people gaining weight. The chart cannot show that low income causes higher BMI. For example, getting older could also raise BMI.

Before believing or sharing a claim about income and weight, check whether it compares income groups and uses data that represents all Americans.

Source: Prepared classroom snapshot derived from CDC/NCHS NHANES public-use data. Results are descriptive and unweighted.
